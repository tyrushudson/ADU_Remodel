Hudson ADU Bath Proposal

A reworked bathroom and hallway for aging in place. The bathroom keeps its walls and is replanned inside them. The hallway grows from 3 feet to 5 feet by pushing the closet row 2 feet into the nook. Both bedrooms, their closets and their doors stay where the architect drew them. For reference, see the layout as drawn and the kitchen plan.

What changes

  1. Hallway widens to 5 feet. A wheelchair can turn around anywhere in it, and two people can walk side by side.
  2. Bath door becomes a 3'-0" pocket door. It opens onto the clear floor, cannot be blocked by a fall, and gives about 34" of clear width.
  3. Curbless shower, 3'-0" by 5'-4". Open along the long side behind a curtain, with a fold-down seat, a hand shower on a slide bar, a drain along the hall-side wall and two grab bars.
  4. Toilet turns onto the right wall. It sits 18" off the rear wall with a fixed grab bar on that wall, a fold-up bar on the open side, and more than 3 feet of floor beside it for a walker, wheelchair or helper.
  5. Wall-hung sink with open knee space, usable seated, and a 15" linen tower beside it in place of the linen cabinet.
  6. Laundry goes side by side. Two front-loaders in a 5-foot closet across from the bath door, so every control and both doors are within reach.
  7. Water heater stays on the right, now against the outside wall, behind a 2'-6" door.
  8. Opening to the nook moves to the left and widens from 3'-2" to 3'-6". It lines up with the living room and the front door.
  9. Bedroom doors are not moved. Swing-clear hinges on the existing 2'-8" doors add about 2" of clear width without touching the frames.

What stays the same

  • Both bedrooms, their closets and their door locations.
  • The bathroom's four walls. Only the fixtures, the door and the window position change.
  • The outside walls, the deck, the French door and the deck-side window.
  • The kitchen. The counters, appliances and the 7'-9" aisle are untouched.
  • The attic access in the hall ceiling.

What it costs and what to check

  • The nook loses 2 feet. At about 6 feet deep it still fits a 5-foot turning circle and a table for two to four, but it is a breakfast area more than a dining room. A 4'-6" hall gives 6 inches back.
  • The closet walls look like bracing. Sheet 5 tags several of them "BP", which marks braced wall panels. Moving them needs the architect or engineer to rework the bracing.
  • The toilet drain moves about 5 feet. The crawlspace makes that straightforward, but it is new plumbing.
  • The bath window moves. The new 2'-0" window shifts toward the toilet end with its sill at about 48", clear of the mirror and the grab bar.
  • The turning circle relies on the sink. It only works with a wall-hung sink and nothing stored underneath.
  • Curbless needs a recessed floor. The shower floor framing has to drop or be sloped so water stays in. Decide this before the subfloor goes down.
  • Add backing now. Plywood behind every grab bar, the fold-down seat and the wall-hung sink, while the studs are open.

This is a concept sketch, not a construction document. It is built on dimensions scaled from a scan of sheet 5 of the architect's permit set, so every figure is approximate and the layout needs the architect to confirm it fits, meets code and works structurally.
