Hudson ADU New Layout

A new interior for the whole cottage inside the same outside walls. The idea is to delete the closet row in the middle of the house. The laundry and water heater move into a deeper bathroom, which leaves room for a 5-foot hall and a dining area nearly the size the architect drew. The kitchen keeps its shape. The plan matches the 3D walkthrough. Earlier pages: the smaller bath-and-hall change, which leaves the bedrooms alone, and the kitchen plan.

What changes from the architect's sheet

  1. Primary bedroom in the right wing. Its door moves to the corner nearest the bathroom and widens to 3'-0". The built-in comes out, which leaves a 5-foot turning circle just inside the door and 3'-6" or more on both sides of a queen bed.
  2. Bathroom deepens from 5'-4" to 7'-6". Curbless shower in the rear corner with a seat, toilet with a grab bar each side, a vanity that matches the kitchen cabinets, 3'-0" pocket door. The 5-foot circle clears every fixture.
  3. Laundry moves into the bathroom. Side-by-side front-loaders on the left wall with shelves above for linens.
  4. Water heater closet in the bathroom, behind a louvered door, with all the plumbing now in one room.
  5. The closet row is removed. In its place is a 5-foot clear hall, open to the dining area on one side.
  6. Dining area about 7'-5" deep, with room for a table for four and a clear path beside it.
  7. Second bedroom door moves to the living-room side and widens to 3'-0". It is about 8 feet from the bathroom door.
  8. Coat closet removed. The living room opens 10 feet wide to the hall and dining area. Coats go in a cabinet with a bench beside the front door.
  9. Deck door from the dining area widens to 3'-0", a left-hand inswing with a low threshold, hinged on the kitchen side.
  10. French doors from the primary bedroom to the deck. A 6-foot outswing pair replaces the optional 8-foot sliding door.
  11. Kitchen windows and cooking. Three windows over the front counter, 24", 30" and 24", with the middle one over the sink. An induction cooktop with a built-in oven below replaces the range.

What it costs and what to check

  • Structure. Sheet 5 tags several closet-row walls "BP", for braced wall panels. This layout removes that row and widens the opening between the living room and the middle of the house to about 10 feet. Both need the architect or engineer to rework the bracing and size a beam.
  • Less separation. The bathroom door and the primary bedroom door open onto a hall that is open to the dining area. The architect's closet row screened them.
  • Laundry in the bathroom. It runs while the room is in use, and the dryer should be a ventless heat-pump model or vent through the roof.
  • Water heater in the bathroom. A heat pump water heater needs a set volume of air and blows cool air. Check the maker's clearances and duct its exhaust outside.
  • All bathroom plumbing moves. Shower and toilet go to the right wall, the sink shifts, and the window moves to the shower end.
  • The vanity is a cabinet. It has no open knee space, so the sink cannot be used from a wheelchair. A wall-hung sink would allow that.
  • Less closed storage. Three closets become shelves and one cabinet. The primary bedroom closet stays the size the architect drew.
  • Second bedroom opens off the living room. That suits a caregiver or guest. It is less private if both parents later want their own room.
  • Getting in is still by steps. The elevations show steps at the porch and the deck. That is outside the footprint and still needs solving.

This is a concept sketch, not a construction document. It is built on a scan of sheet 5 of the architect's permit set. Overall dimensions and bedroom sizes are the ones written on the sheet; everything else is scaled from the scan and is approximate. Furniture is shown only to test fit. The layout needs the architect to confirm it fits, meets code and works structurally.
