What changes from the architect's sheet

  1. Hallway widens to 5 feet, enough to turn a wheelchair around anywhere in it.
  2. Bath door becomes a 3'-0" pocket door with about 34" of clear width.
  3. Curbless shower, 3'-0" by 5'-4", open along the long side, with a fold-down seat and two grab bars.
  4. Toilet turns onto the right wall, with a grab bar each side and over 3 feet of floor beside it.
  5. Wall-hung sink with open knee space and a 15" linen tower beside it.
  6. Laundry goes side by side in a 5-foot closet across from the bath door.
  7. Water heater stays on the right, now against the outside wall.
  8. Opening to the nook moves to the left and widens to 3'-6", in line with the living room and front door.
  9. Bedroom doors are not moved. Swing-clear hinges add about 2" of clear width to each.

What it costs and what to check

  • The nook loses 2 feet. At about 6 feet deep it fits a table for two to four. A 4'-6" hall gives 6 inches back.
  • The closet walls look like bracing. Sheet 5 tags several "BP", for braced wall panels. Moving them needs the architect or engineer to rework the bracing.
  • Plumbing moves. The toilet drain shifts about 5 feet, and the laundry and water heater connections shift 2 to 4 feet.
  • The bath turning circle relies on the sink. It only works with a wall-hung sink and nothing stored underneath.
  • Curbless needs a recessed floor. Decide this before the subfloor goes down.
  • Getting in is still by steps. The elevations show steps at both the porch and the deck. This plan does not change that.

This is a concept sketch, not a construction document. It is redrawn from a scan of sheet 5 of the architect's permit set. Overall dimensions and room sizes are the ones written on the sheet.
